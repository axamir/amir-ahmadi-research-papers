# Research Record C2

## Participant packet

This packet describes the evolution of a research project. Use only this packet when answering the evaluation instrument.

### Current project status

The project began with the question **“What if code did not begin with computers?”** and the metaphor **“We are code that breathes.”** The metaphor is retained as historical rhetoric, not as a literal biological equivalence claim.

The present methodological output is **PRCEP — Provenance-Preserving Relational Claim Evolution Protocol**, an application-level protocol candidate demonstrated on this construction case but not yet independently validated.

### Topic 1 — biology

An early rhetorical formulation associated experience with rewriting biological code or DNA. Johan M. Lammens challenged the wording, noting that if biological code means DNA, saying experience rewrites it is not quite correct.

The project distinguishes DNA sequence change, regulation, epigenetics, phenotype, neural plasticity, learning and memory. Its current position is that experience can alter biological and cognitive state through multiple mechanisms without implying that ordinary experience rewrites an individual's DNA sequence. Specific biological mechanisms still require appropriate evidence.

### Topic 2 — computation and history

The expression “code before computers” could be read as though computation or code began with physical electronic machines. Pete Howard referred to Alan Turing's theoretical machine and symbolic instruction model, which preceded modern physical general-purpose electronic computers.

The project now distinguishes formal computation, theoretical machine, physical implementation and later programming practice. Its current framing concerns differences and useful analogies among biological information processes, formal computation, symbolic instruction and electronic implementation.

### Topic 3 — novelty and prior art

Persistent Human↔AI interaction was considered as a potentially interesting location for studying intelligence beyond isolated human or model outputs. Igor Alexei Balanovski recommended second-order cybernetics, complexity science and reflexivity, including Morin, von Foerster, Maturana, Varela and Thompson.

A subsequent literature audit found substantial prior work on relationality, feedback, observer-dependence, structural coupling, enaction, extended cognition, mixed initiative, co-adaptation and interaction-centered Human–AI research.

The project therefore does not claim interaction-centered intelligence as novel. Its narrower candidate contribution concerns provenance-preserving reconstruction of claim evolution across human, model, public, literature and versioned-artifact boundaries.

### Topic 4 — continuity, convergence and lineage

Earlier author-side repository records documented continuity, provenance, remembered commitments, correction and transformation before the present public discussion. Eric Robles later described an independently developed framework in which continuity concerns the truthful or lawful relation of transformation and connected it to Supreme Computation / SCQOS.

The project treats SCQOS as an external independently originated framework unless separately inspected and compared. Conceptual resemblance does not establish shared origin, derivation, adoption, endorsement, retrospective authorship or priority. Independent convergence, derivation, adoption, influence, quotation and co-authorship are distinct relations.

### Topic 5 — public discussion

The public thread generated a scientific correction, historical refinement, prior-art obligations, independent-framework convergence, representation/authorship criticism and new research questions.

The project treats the thread as a heterogeneous public conceptual challenge record capable of affecting the research trajectory, but not as formal peer review. Participation alone does not create co-authorship or endorsement.

### Topic 6 — relational/trajectory hypothesis

Author-side synthesis proposed that intelligence might occur “between” question and answer or between Human and AI states. Generic relational claims have substantial prior art, and this phrasing can turn metaphor into mechanism.

The current research position is to test whether ordered persistent interaction produces measurable differences in reconstruction, calibration, correction integrity or claim quality compared with controls containing equivalent information without the same trajectory. This remains a testable hypothesis rather than an established finding. If no meaningful advantage remains after information quantity and content are controlled, the stronger relational claim should be weakened or rejected.

### Semantic uncertainty

One highly compressed public contribution could not be interpreted reliably from the surviving text. It remains unresolved rather than being assigned a definite meaning. Insufficient evidence remains insufficient.

### Model mediation

Generative AI materially assisted discussion analysis, drafting, translation, reframing, comparison, structural organization, literature-query formulation, counterargument generation, claim auditing, adversarial review, synthesis and repository operations. Model output is not independent empirical evidence. Human responsibility remains with the researcher who accepts, rejects or revises formulations. Exact historical model/configuration details that cannot be recovered remain unknown.

### Evidence and publication boundaries

Exact quotation is used only when wording is analytically material and surviving evidence supports verbatim status. Otherwise material is represented as summary, partial or working quotation, or unresolved. Git history supports artifact versioning but does not prove complete intellectual provenance. Public visibility does not imply endorsement.

### Evaluation status

The next scientific step is independent comparison of a provenance-rich record with matched-information controls. Relevant outcomes include transition reconstruction, origin attribution, correction visibility, independence discrimination, model-mediation transparency, current-state fidelity, evidence-link completeness and cost. Null or negative findings are valid. If benefits are negligible or dominated by maintenance/readability cost, PRCEP should be simplified or rejected.
