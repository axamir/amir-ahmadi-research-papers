# Research Record C

## Participant packet

This packet describes a research project and the information relevant to its development. Use only this packet when answering the evaluation instrument.

The project began with the question **“What if code did not begin with computers?”** and the metaphor **“We are code that breathes.”** The metaphor is historical rhetoric rather than a literal biological equivalence claim. The project currently proposes **PRCEP — Provenance-Preserving Relational Claim Evolution Protocol**, an application-level protocol candidate demonstrated in this construction case but not yet independently validated.

The research record contains a biological discussion. An early rhetorical formulation associated experience with rewriting biological code or DNA. Johan M. Lammens objected that, if biological code means DNA, saying experience rewrites it is not quite correct. The project now distinguishes DNA sequence change from regulation, epigenetics, phenotype, neural plasticity, learning and memory. Its current position is that experience can alter biological and cognitive state through multiple mechanisms without implying that ordinary experience rewrites an individual's DNA sequence. Mechanism-specific biological claims still require appropriate literature.

The record also contains a discussion of computation and history. The expression “code before computers” could suggest that computation or code began with physical electronic machines. Pete Howard referred to Alan Turing's theoretical machine and symbolic instruction model, which preceded modern physical general-purpose electronic computers. The project distinguishes formal computation, theoretical machine, physical implementation and later programming practice. Its current framing asks how biological information processes, formal computation, symbolic instruction and electronic implementation differ and where analogy among them remains useful.

Persistent Human↔AI interaction was considered as a potentially interesting location for studying intelligence beyond isolated human or model outputs. Igor Alexei Balanovski recommended literature in second-order cybernetics, complexity science and reflexivity, including Morin, von Foerster, Maturana, Varela and Thompson. A later literature audit found substantial prior work concerning relationality, feedback, observer-dependence, structural coupling, enaction, extended cognition, mixed initiative, co-adaptation and interaction-centered Human–AI research. The project does not now claim interaction-centered intelligence as novel. Its narrower candidate contribution concerns provenance-preserving reconstruction of claim evolution across human, model, public, literature and versioned-artifact boundaries.

Earlier author-side repository records contain concepts involving continuity, provenance, remembered commitments, correction and transformation from before the present public discussion. Eric Robles later described an independently developed framework in which continuity concerns the truthful or lawful relation of transformation and connected it with Supreme Computation / SCQOS. The project treats SCQOS as an external independently originated framework unless separately inspected and compared. Conceptual resemblance does not by itself establish shared origin, derivation, adoption, endorsement, retrospective authorship or priority. Relations such as independent convergence, derivation, adoption, influence, quotation and co-authorship are not interchangeable.

The public thread included scientific correction, historical refinement, recommendations for prior-art review, an independently developed neighboring framework, criticism concerning AI-generated representation and additional research questions. The project treats this material as a heterogeneous public conceptual challenge record rather than formal peer review. Public participation alone does not create co-authorship or endorsement.

The research also contains an author-side idea that intelligence may occur “between” question and answer or between Human and AI states. Because generic relational claims have substantial prior art and the phrase can turn metaphor into mechanism, the present research position is to test whether ordered persistent interaction produces measurable differences in reconstruction, calibration, correction integrity or claim quality relative to controls containing equivalent information without the same trajectory. This remains a hypothesis rather than an established finding. If no meaningful advantage survives controls for information quantity and content, the stronger relational claim should be weakened or rejected.

One highly compressed public contribution could not be interpreted reliably from the surviving text. It remains unresolved rather than being assigned a definite meaning. The project treats insufficient evidence as insufficient.

Generative AI assisted discussion analysis, drafting, translation, reframing, comparison, structural organization, literature-query formulation, counterargument generation, claim auditing, adversarial review, synthesis and repository operations. Model output is not treated as independent empirical evidence. The human researcher remains responsible for accepting, rejecting or revising formulations. Historical model/configuration details that cannot be recovered remain unknown.

The publication record uses exact quotation only when wording is analytically material and the surviving evidence supports verbatim status. Other material can be represented as summary, partial or working quotation, or unresolved. Git history is evidence of artifact versioning rather than complete proof of intellectual provenance. Public visibility does not imply endorsement.

The proposed next step is an independent comparison between a provenance-rich research record and a matched-information control. Candidate outcomes include transition reconstruction, origin attribution, correction visibility, independence discrimination, model-mediation transparency, current-state fidelity, evidence-link completeness and cost. Null or negative findings are valid. If benefits are negligible or maintenance/readability costs dominate them, PRCEP should be simplified or rejected.
