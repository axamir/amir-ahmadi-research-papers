# Literature Gap Matrix

**Document:** ARP-WCB-2026-01  
**Status:** active prior-art audit  
**Audit date:** 2026-08-14

This matrix is designed to *reduce* novelty claims until a defensible residual contribution remains.

## 1. Extended mind / active externalism

**Clark & Chalmers (1998; reprinted in Menary ed.)** already argue that a human organism can be reciprocally linked with an external entity in a coupled system and that external components may play an active causal role in cognition.

**Pressure on this paper:** “Human↔AI as a coupled system” is not novel by itself.

**Residual question:** generative AI is not a passive notebook. It produces novel linguistic responses, can challenge the user, and changes interaction trajectories. Does this create measurable epistemic dynamics not captured by generic coupling language?

## 2. Coupling–constitution objection

**Adams & Aizawa** argue that coupling an external process to a cognitive agent does not automatically make that process constitutive of cognition—the coupling–constitution fallacy.

**Pressure:** the paper must never infer “the relation is cognitive” merely from reciprocal influence.

**Design response:** PRTM-0.1 treats relation as a *candidate explanatory variable/unit of analysis*, not as demonstrated cognitive substance.

## 3. Cognitive integration

**Richard Menary** distinguishes simple functional parity/coupling from deeper complementarity and integration of internal and external vehicles into a cognitive whole.

**Pressure:** integration and complementarity are prior concepts.

**Residual question:** can provenance-preserving longitudinal interaction metrics distinguish shallow tool use from path-dependent research co-development?

## 4. Operational conditions for extension

**David Kirsh (2019)** proposes conditions including temporal tightness, transparency, ownership/non-independence of external parts, and bidirectional interaction.

**Pressure:** bidirectionality alone is insufficient; generative AI may also violate assumptions such as external-part non-independence.

**Opportunity:** explicitly compare generative AI collaboration against Kirsh’s conditions rather than casually calling it an extended mind.

## 5. Embedded vs embodied vs extended cognition

**Robert Rupert** distinguishes extended cognition from embedded cognition (organism remains bounded but depends heavily on environment) and embodied cognition (non-neural body materially shapes cognition).

**Pressure:** observed dependence on AI does not establish cognitive extension.

**Design response:** manuscript should remain neutral among “extended,” “embedded,” “hybrid,” and “coupled epistemic” interpretations until evidence discriminates them.

## 6. Extended epistemology / technologically extended knowledge

**Orestis Palermos** examines knowledge produced through close coupling of biological and technological resources, including questions of justification, trust, ethics and distributed/extended agents.

**Pressure:** “epistemic partnership” and technology-mediated knowledge are not new territories.

**Residual question:** can explicit provenance/correction history improve justification and epistemic credit assignment in generative-AI collaboration?

## 7. AI and extended conceptual capacities

Contemporary philosophy of AI has already considered a corollary of extended-mind views in which AI systems may contribute conceptual competencies to human cognitive processes.

**Pressure:** AI as an active cognitive resource is prior art.

**Residual question:** our contribution must concern traceable transformation, longitudinal interaction, attribution, correction, and empirical comparison—not the mere presence of AI in cognition.

## 8. Interaction-centered intelligence — very close contemporary prior art

**Nicholas Davis (2026), “Interaction-Centered Intelligence: Toward an Interaction-Based Theory of Human-AI Co-Creation”** explicitly proposes interaction as a primary unit of analysis for co-creative AI, drawing on distributed cognition, embodiment, enaction, participatory sense-making, HCI and computational creativity. It emphasizes interaction trajectories, coordination, adaptive participation and drift over time.

**Pressure: CRITICAL.** A broad claim that “intelligence is in the interaction” is no longer defensible as this paper’s novelty.

**Required response:** cite and compare directly. Narrow our contribution to provenance-preserving research trajectories, claim-state transitions, adversarial public input, attribution, correction history, and falsifiable ablation tests.

## 9. Human–AI coevolution dynamics — close contemporary prior art

**Zhou, Luo & Chen (2026)** propose a formal framework of long-term human–AI interaction as a self-organizing social cognitive system, using multi-timescale dynamics, relational attractors, social memory, developmental transitions and empirical conversational data.

**Pressure: CRITICAL.** Long-term interaction, coevolution, relational dynamics and formalization are not unique contributions.

**Difference to test:** this project is not primarily a theory of social intelligence or emotional relationship formation. It targets epistemic research transformation and reconstructable provenance. That distinction must be demonstrated, not asserted.

## 10. Human–AI epistemic partnership — close contemporary prior art

**Xiaoming Zhai (2026)** develops Human–AI Epistemic Partnership Theory around negotiated epistemic authority, agency, accountability and calibration cycles.

**Pressure: HIGH.** “Epistemic partnership” and repeated trust/skepticism calibration are already explicit.

**Residual opportunity:** formal proposition-level provenance and correction lineage may offer a different operational layer, especially for research artifacts and public intellectual development.

## 11. Bidirectional human–AI alignment

**ACM CHI 2026 workshop activity** explicitly frames alignment as reciprocal co-adaptation in which humans and AI evolve through interaction, rather than only AI adapting to human values.

**Pressure:** reciprocal co-adaptation is an active research program, not a new observation.

**Residual opportunity:** distinguish value alignment from epistemic/provenance continuity.

## 12. Hybrid cognitive systems / double mediation

Recent work on **Hybrid Cognitive Systems** argues for meaningful human–AI cognitive mediation without requiring artificial consciousness and explicitly positions itself between easy extended-mind attribution and conservative denial of integration.

**Pressure:** “integration without AI consciousness” is also prior art.

**Residual opportunity:** PRTM can remain ontologically modest and focus on auditable transition structure rather than deciding whether the hybrid system literally constitutes a mind.

# Gap after pressure testing

The following claims should be abandoned as novelty claims:

- interaction as a unit of analysis;
- reciprocal human–AI adaptation;
- long-term coevolution;
- distributed/extended cognition;
- hybrid cognition without AI consciousness;
- epistemic partnership;
- feedback/recursion/relationality in general;
- provenance or governance in general.

## Candidate residual contribution

A narrower contribution may remain at the intersection of these areas:

> **A versioned, provenance-preserving methodology for studying how research propositions transform during persistent Human↔generative-AI collaboration, where every material claim can retain origin, external challenge, evidence class, correction history, uncertainty, and responsibility; combined with ablation tests designed to determine whether interaction trajectory adds explanatory value beyond equal information presented without that trajectory.**

Potentially distinctive components:

1. **Proposition state machine** preserving proposed/supported/refined/corrected/rejected/external states.
2. **Contributor independence ledger** preventing convergence from being mistaken for shared lineage.
3. **Public adversarial input** incorporated as provenance-bearing transformation rather than post-publication noise.
4. **History/provenance/order/volume ablations** for testing relational contribution.
5. **Research artifact that is itself the longitudinal record** of the thesis changing under criticism.
6. Explicit separation of **AI mediation** from **human publication responsibility**.

# Novelty threshold

The final paper should not say “we introduce a new theory of relational intelligence” unless a later systematic review supports that statement.

A safer target is:

> **We introduce and document a provenance-preserving research protocol and formal transition model for auditing persistent human–generative-AI co-development of claims.**

Even this formulation remains provisional until a broader systematic search is completed.
