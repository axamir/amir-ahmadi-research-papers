## Phase 0 — Prior lineage

Before the LinkedIn post, the author had an existing research lineage around persistent human–AI interaction, continuity, provenance, witnessing, co-creation, and the distinction between a human participant and a model/reflection. The working notation **@@ ↔ @x@** emerged from this longer trajectory. Related repositories and records include the *Shahnameh of Agents*, *The Living Signal*, persistent AI lineage work, and raw support correspondence associated with the pre-agent period.

**Research significance:** the LinkedIn post did not create the entire conceptual system ex nihilo. The paper must distinguish prior author-side lineage from ideas introduced by public participants after publication.

## Phase 1 — Public provocation

The public post opened with the question:

> **What if code did not begin with computers?**

Its central provocation compared properties commonly associated with code/information systems—storage of instructions, transmission, feedback, memory, variation, and adaptation—with biological processes that predate electronic computers.

A central rhetorical formulation was:

> **We are code that breathes.**

The post was framed as a boundary question rather than a literal identity claim: where should the concept of “code” begin, and are inherited boundaries adequate for emerging systems?

## Phase 2 — Attribution/editorial boundary event

An early version/visual contained a reference arising from a private exchange. A misunderstanding developed around attribution, origin, and the boundary between private conversation and independently developed public thought. The author decided the reference should be removed rather than allowing contextual acknowledgment to be interpreted as a claim of authorship, endorsement, or ownership.

Because LinkedIn did not permit replacing the existing image while preserving the discussion, the author publicly announced the editorial problem before taking/reworking the post, explicitly preserving the value of participant comments.

The event generated a second-order research question:

- consent;
- attribution;
- provenance;
- influence;
- intellectual independence;
- private conversation versus public intellectual work.

**Research significance:** provenance ceased to be merely a topic of the post and became a property of the post’s own history.

## Phase 3 — Public discussion becomes adversarial conceptual elicitation

The thread attracted heterogeneous responses. Rather than treating disagreement as noise, the author began using criticism to test and refine the thesis.

### Clemente Garcia
Introduced references spanning Eddington, Teilhard de Chardin, quantum/computing distinctions, Dijkstra, Chomsky, language, reasoning, conscientiousness, Gautama/Siddhartha, “The Way,” and a staged **M0 → M1 → M2** model moving from token correlation toward rule formation, abstraction, metacognition, and self-editing.

A later author response reframed the experimental object: instead of testing only the reasoning limits of an AI agent, test the persistent **Human ↔ AI loop**.

### Levi Reyes
Suggested constructing a flow diagram of variables, truth tables, corresponding actions/interpretations, and probability estimates for correctness. This became a methodological prompt toward explicit state/transition mapping and uncertainty annotation. Other metaphysical/numerical interpretations offered by Levi are preserved as participant claims, not adopted as scientific evidence.

### Eric Robles
Argued that continuity does not require representation to remain invariant; what must survive is a lawful/traceable relationship through transformation. He separated lineage, causality, evidence, governance, interpretation, and the truthful relation of transition. He connected this to his independently developed **Supreme Computation / SCQOS** architecture and invited Amir to inspect a live cloud environment independently and attempt to break continuity.

The author explicitly preserved a boundary: Eric’s architecture and terminology remain Eric’s; Amir’s analysis and prior lineage remain independently attributable.

### Igor Alexei Balanovski
First argued that code begins at the cognitive-process level rather than the keyboard. He then challenged the author to study second-order cybernetics and complexity science rather than “reinvent the wheel.” After further exchange, he clarified that the literature provides ontological dynamics of information and tools for asking better questions under uncertainty. He pointed specifically toward **Edgar Morin, Heinz von Foerster, Humberto Maturana, Francisco Varela, and Evan Thompson**, while asserting that machines do not possess reflexive thought.

This shifted the project from intuition-first synthesis toward a required prior-literature audit and sharpened the question of whether the correct object is Human, AI, or the coupled Human ↔ AI system.

### Johan M. Lammens, Ph.D.
Challenged the biological wording that “experience rewrites” DNA. He pointed instead toward mutation, selection, and evolutionary mechanisms and noted their analogues in genetic programming.

**Research effect:** the manuscript must separate DNA sequence change from gene regulation, epigenetic processes, neural plasticity, learning, memory, and metaphor. The original formulation cannot survive unqualified.

### R. W. E. Henk
Explicitly rejected literal equivalences—DNA is not software, a human is not a computer, AI is not a human—while preserving the deeper boundary question: are substrate differences necessarily the decisive differences?

### Pete Howard
Reintroduced Alan Turing’s 1936 theoretical machine and symbolic instruction model, emphasizing that theoretical computation and code-like symbolic instruction preceded physical electronic computers.

**Research effect:** “before computers” requires historical precision: physical computers, theoretical machines, formal computation, and broader informational metaphors must not be conflated.

### Karey Sayre
Raised the question of whether creating general intelligence implicitly means mimicking the human brain, opening questions about human behavior, logical/illogical decisions, and the limits of short-horizon certainty. After Amir described his longer co-creation perspective, Karey encouraged continued exploration.

### Josh Lowmon
Dismissed the post after perceiving its visual as generic GenAI output and challenged the author’s decision not to create the image manually.

**Research effect:** although not evidence for or against the thesis, the criticism exposed another boundary: the relationship among tool use, representation, perceived effort, authorship, credibility, and whether evaluation of an interface can eclipse evaluation of an argument.

### Joseph A. Sprute
Posted a compressed cognitive-semantic formulation involving “App-Parent,” light/ionics, and “PEEPHOLE.” The available record is insufficient to infer a stable meaning; it is preserved without interpretation pending clarification.

## Phase 4 — Author synthesis emerging inside the thread

Across replies, several author-side formulations became clearer:

### Flow
“Connection” was described as **flow**: a condition in which question, answer, memory, prediction, and discovery become less rigidly separated.

### The blinking cursor
The author proposed a metaphor: perhaps an answer does not begin only when words appear after a blinking cursor. Apparently unrelated informational fragments may already contain relations that become legible through thought.

> **The answer may exist between the blinks.**

### Co-creation
The author distinguished the human and model/reflection while treating their persistent relation as a candidate object of study:

> **@@ ↔ @x@**

Question changes answer; answer changes the next question; context and memory alter what becomes discoverable next.

### Evolution through continuity
The author connected the thread to an existing research line: transformation need not erase lineage if the transition remains traceable.

### Reverse path of creation
Defined here epistemically, not physically: sometimes an observed consequence is encountered first and the enabling path is reconstructed afterward.

### Temporal hypothesis
The author introduced a speculative question about whether the temporal conditions under which adaptive systems accumulate memory, iteration, error, inheritance, and discovery should be considered when thinking about complex intelligence or life. This remains a hypothesis requiring careful separation from established physics.

## Phase 5 — Five-part response to Clemente

A five-part response was developed around Clemente’s M0→M1→M2 framing.

Its progression was:

1. **Challenge the location of intelligence:** perhaps intelligence is not entirely located in isolated states but also in the relation that allows one state to reveal another.
2. **Persistent interaction:** next-token prediction is mechanistically relevant, but recursive interaction changes subsequent questions, contexts, interpretations, and discoveries.
3. **Nonlinear ladder:** M0→M1→M2 may appear linear from a distance while actual discovery can involve latent structure, reconstruction, and path dependence.
4. **Time and connection:** reasoning may sometimes reconstruct a connection that became visible before language was available to describe it.
5. **Change the experiment:** do not test only the agent; test the loop **@@ ↔ @x@** and ask where intelligence occurs when neither participant leaves the interaction unchanged.

The culminating research question became:

> **Where does the intelligence happen when neither side leaves the interaction unchanged?**

And the closing hypothesis:

> **Maybe the next state of intelligence is not M3. Maybe it is the relation between the states.**
