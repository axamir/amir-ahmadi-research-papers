# References & Prior-Art Spine

**Document:** ARP-WCB-2026-01  
**Status:** release-candidate bibliography; citation-by-citation alignment required before archival publication

This bibliography constrains the paper's novelty and terminology. Participant comments remain in the discussion record; scholarly prior art enters here only after the relevant work has been identified.

## Formal computation

### Turing, Alan M. (1936)
**“On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem.”** *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society*, s2-42(1), 230–265.

Turing's abstract machine predates modern electronic general-purpose computers. “Code before computers” must therefore distinguish formal computation, symbolic instruction, and physical implementation.

## Complexity and reflexivity

### Morin, Edgar (1990)
**Introduction à la pensée complexe.** ESF.

Constrains reductive attempts to collapse biology, computation, cognition, and information into a single vocabulary.

### von Foerster, Heinz (2003)
**Understanding Understanding: Essays on Cybernetics and Cognition.** Springer.

Constrains novelty claims around observer participation, recursion, reflexivity, and systems observing systems.

## Autopoiesis, embodiment, enaction, and dynamics

### Maturana, Humberto R., & Varela, Francisco J. (1980)
**Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living.** D. Reidel / Springer. DOI: 10.1007/978-94-009-8947-4.

Constrains claims that cognition-before-language or relational/systemic cognition originate in this project.

### Varela, Francisco J., Thompson, Evan, & Rosch, Eleanor (1991; revised ed. 2017)
**The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience.** MIT Press.

Constrains novelty around embodiment, enaction, lived experience, relation, and environment-dependent cognition.

### Thelen, Esther, & Smith, Linda B. (1994)
**A Dynamic Systems Approach to the Development of Cognition and Action.** MIT Press. DOI: 10.7551/mitpress/2524.001.0001.

Provides a major prior-art boundary for process-oriented, developmental, nonlinear, and self-organizing accounts of cognition and action. The present manuscript cannot treat temporally unfolding cognition as a new conceptual move.

## Extended and distributed cognition

### Clark, Andy, & Chalmers, David J. (1998)
**“The Extended Mind.”** *Analysis*, 58(1), 7–19. DOI: 10.1093/analys/58.1.7.

Develops active externalism: environmental components can play an active role in cognitive processes. This is a direct prior-art boundary against broad claims that cognition or intelligence has newly been located beyond the isolated individual.

### Hutchins, Edwin (1995)
**Cognition in the Wild.** MIT Press.

Required comparison for socially and materially distributed cognition. The manuscript must not equate Human–AI coupling with a wholly unprecedented move beyond individual cognition.

## Mixed-initiative Human–AI interaction

### Horvitz, Eric (1999)
**“Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces.”** *Proceedings of CHI '99*, 159–166. DOI: 10.1145/302979.303030.

Establishes a strong prior-art boundary for collaboration between users and intelligent services, including uncertainty, timing, user goals, automated action, and shared problem solving. PRCEP therefore cannot claim that coupling human judgment and automated assistance is itself novel.

## Belief revision

### Alchourrón, Carlos E., Gärdenfors, Peter, & Makinson, David (1985)
**“On the Logic of Theory Change: Partial Meet Contraction and Revision Functions.”** *The Journal of Symbolic Logic*, 50(2), 510–530. DOI: 10.2307/2274239.

Formalizes contraction and revision of theories. PRCEP must therefore not claim invention of belief or theory revision. Its candidate contribution concerns inspectable provenance of hybrid research transitions rather than a new general logic of revision.

## Provenance and scientific claim/evidence representation

### Lebo, Timothy, Sahoo, Satya, McGuinness, Deborah, et al. (2013)
**PROV-O: The PROV Ontology.** W3C Recommendation, 30 April 2013.

PROV-O provides a general ontology for representing provenance through entities, activities, agents, and relations. PRCEP must be framed as an application-level protocol and reconstruction layer, not as the invention of provenance representation.

### Clark, Tim; Ciccarese, Paolo N.; & Goble, Carole A. (2014)
**“Micropublications: a semantic model for claims, evidence, arguments and annotations in biomedical communications.”** *Journal of Biomedical Semantics*, 5:28. DOI: 10.1186/2041-1480-5-28.

Micropublications is a direct prior-art boundary for representing scientific claims together with evidence, argumentation, annotations, support, similarity, challenge, discussion, and disagreement. PRCEP therefore must not claim novelty merely for combining claims, evidence, provenance, and challenge. Its narrower candidate contribution concerns longitudinal transition semantics in persistent Human–Generative-AI/public research: superseded claim states, explicit transition decisions and reasons, model-mediation roles, independent-convergence lineage semantics, and reconstruction-oriented evaluation against matched-information controls.

## Biology, experience, plasticity, and memory

### Lubin, Farah D. (2011)
**“Epigenetic mechanisms in experience-driven memory formation and behavior.”** *Epigenomics*, 3(6), 713–731. PMID: 22126252; PMCID: PMC3235677.

Learning and experience can trigger dynamic epigenetic regulation associated with transcription and memory. These mechanisms do not justify the simplified statement that ordinary experience rewrites DNA sequence.

### Leighton, L. J.; Ke, K.; Zajaczkowski, E. L.; Edmunds, J.; Spitale, R. C.; & Bredy, T. W. (2018)
**“Experience-dependent neural plasticity, learning, and memory in the era of epitranscriptomics.”** *Genes, Brain and Behavior*, 17(3), e12426. DOI: 10.1111/gbb.12426.

Supports separating DNA sequence, epigenetic regulation, epitranscriptomic change, gene expression, neural plasticity, learning, and memory rather than collapsing them into a single notion of biological “rewriting.”

### Kim, Somi, & Kaang, Bong-Kiun (2017)
**“Epigenetic regulation and chromatin remodeling in learning and memory.”** *Experimental & Molecular Medicine*, 49, e281.

Supports the distinction between changes in genome regulation and changes in nucleotide sequence.

## Information and meaning

### Shannon, Claude E. (1948)
**“A Mathematical Theory of Communication.”** *Bell System Technical Journal*, 27, 379–423 and 623–656.

Required boundary between mathematical information/communication and semantic meaning. The manuscript must not slide from information-bearing structure to meaning without an explicit additional argument.

## Evolutionary computation

### Koza, John R. (1992)
**Genetic Programming: On the Programming of Computers by Means of Natural Selection.** MIT Press.

Demonstrates that evolutionary mechanisms and metaphors have long been operationalized computationally. The paper must distinguish biological evolution, evolutionary computation, and metaphorical “evolution through continuity.”

## Artificial metacognition and operational self-monitoring

### Shakarian, Paulo (2026)
**“Toward Artificial Metacognition.”** *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence*, 40(48), 41000–41005. DOI: 10.1609/aaai.v40i48.42135.

Reviews AI systems that self-monitor and/or regulate resources. This prevents the manuscript from treating operational self-monitoring or machine metacognition as evidence that this project discovered machine reflexivity, consciousness, or self-awareness.

## AI-assisted authorship and responsibility

### International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE)
**Recommendations: Use of Artificial Intelligence in Publishing — Use of AI by Authors.** Current recommendation accessed for the 2026 manuscript audit.

Requires disclosure of AI-assisted technologies used in manuscript production, rejects AI tools as authors because they cannot assume authorship responsibility, and keeps humans responsible for accuracy, integrity, originality, attribution, and review of generated material. This supports the manuscript's separation of model mediation from authorship and evidence.
