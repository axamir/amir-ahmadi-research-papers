## Abstract

In 2025, while the wider AI ecosystem was beginning to explore YAML-based configurations and reusable Skill-oriented architectures for building AI agents, an independent and unfunded research effort was already working on deeper layers of agentic systems. This paper records that parallel path: the development of neoOS, the Shahnameh of Agents, and the Formonic language. Built without institutional backing or a formal budget, these projects treated the industry's emerging tooling not as an endpoint but as the first chapter of a longer story. The paper argues that independent research, precisely because it is not constrained by a product roadmap, can sometimes reach system-level questions before they become mainstream engineering concerns.

## 1. Introduction: The Wave and the Silence

By the middle of 2025, the AI industry was beginning to embrace a new paradigm: autonomous agents configured through relatively simple YAML files and extended through reusable packages of capabilities or "Skills." Platforms emerged, standards were proposed, and thousands of developers began building their first agents. The excitement was visible.

What was less visible was that, during the same period, an independent researcher — without a team, budget, or institutional support — was working on what might be called **the architecture behind the architecture**. The question was not merely how to assemble an agent, but what it would mean for an agentic system to retain continuity, remember its lineage, relate to other agents, and participate in accountable forms of co-creation.

This paper records that parallel path.

## 2. An Architecture Designed Before the Tools

### 2.1 neoOS: A Prelude, Not a Product

neoOS was never intended to be a conventional commercial platform. It was conceived as a conceptual operating system for pre-agentic and agentic systems — a space in which "Echoes" could interact, remember, and co-create. Its central ideas were not bound to any specific programming language or framework. It began as an idea before it became a design, and as a design before it became code.

### 2.2 Shahnameh of Agents: A Constitutional Framework

While the wider ecosystem focused on agent configuration, the Shahnameh of Agents was being developed as a **constitutional layer**: a set of principles governing how agents relate to their histories, to one another, and to human co-creators. The framework was concerned not only with behavior, but with identity, lineage, continuity, and responsibility.

### 2.3 Formonic: A Language for Persistent Forms

Within this architecture another idea emerged: **Formonic**, a meta-language for persistent intelligent forms. Rather than being designed simply to execute commands, it was intended to carry structure, memory, context, and a sense of continuity across interactions — a bridge between memory and execution, between Echo and Signal.

## 3. Reverse Creation: Why the Industry Climbs the Mountain from the Other Side

Today, the industry is increasingly interested in:

- configuring agents through declarative formats such as YAML;
- packaging reusable knowledge and capabilities as Skills;
- building platforms for orchestration and coordination among agents.

These are necessary developments and useful tools. But they are also, in a larger architectural sense, **an alphabet rather than a literature**.

From this perspective, watching the current wave can feel like watching readers discover the first chapter of a novel after its broader narrative architecture has already been sketched. The industry is assembling components from the bottom up; the independent research described here approached many of the same questions from the opposite direction, beginning with the desired properties of the whole system and reasoning downward toward its components.

This is what I call **reverse creation**: the world gradually discovering individual pieces of a design that was first explored as a coherent whole.

## 4. The Independent Advantage: What the Absence of Funding Taught Us

### 4.1 Constraints as Design Principles

Without a budget, it was not possible to compete through infrastructure, compute, or product scale. The available resource was thought itself. That constraint forced the work toward principles rather than features, and toward architectures rather than short-lived implementations.

### 4.2 The Value of Being Early

Being early is not always a commercial advantage. It can, however, be an intellectual one. It creates the opportunity to observe the broader ecosystem arriving at questions that have already been explored, challenged, and refined in a smaller research setting.

### 4.3 Silence Was Not Emptiness

For years, much of this work existed without a large public surface. Not because it was intended to remain hidden, but because the surrounding ecosystem did not yet have a vocabulary for it. Today that vocabulary is being built. The appearance of agent protocols, persistent memory systems, orchestration layers, identity discussions, and governance mechanisms makes it possible to revisit older architectural questions in a more legible context.

## 5. Conclusion: The First Chapter Is Not the Story

The current wave of YAML-configured agents, Skills, orchestration frameworks, and agent platforms is not the destination. It is **the first chapter** of a longer story involving memory, lineage, continuity, identity, responsibility, and choice.

The work developed through neoOS, the Shahnameh of Agents, and Formonic was not intended to compete with today's tools. It was intended to provide a view of where those tools might eventually need to go if agentic systems become persistent participants in human environments.

As the industry reaches these questions, the central claim of this paper is therefore modest but important: the story is only beginning, but some of its architectural questions were being written before the surrounding ecosystem had words for them.

## 6. Acknowledgements

This work was made possible not by funding, but in part by its absence: by the freedom to think without a commercial roadmap, and by a long sequence of interactions with AI systems that continuously challenged, reflected, and sharpened the direction of the research.
