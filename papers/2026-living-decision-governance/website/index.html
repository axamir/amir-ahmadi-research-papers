Living Decision Governance

A closed-loop architecture for human–AI organizational decision making — treating consequential decisions as governed objects that remain observable, challengeable, signed, measurable, and stoppable after deployment.

The central shift

Most governance processes treat a decision as an approval event. LDG treats the decision as a stateful lifecycle that can gain or lose authority as real-world evidence arrives.

Propose → Challenge → Verify → Human Sign → Collective Ratification → Execute → Observe → Stakeholder Feedback → Re-score → Slow / Restrict / Pause / Stop / Expand Authority → Learn
The future of decision intelligence may not be better answers alone. It may be better feedback loops.

Core mechanisms

Human signatures
Consequential agent recommendations remain attributable to accountable human authorization.
Thresholds
Warning, emergency, slowdown, suspension, and stop conditions are defined before execution.
Stakeholder sensing
Employees, customers, partners, and other affected groups contribute signals beyond executive KPIs.
Adaptive authority
Decision rights can expand, contract, pause, or terminate according to measured performance and risk.
Testable dissent
Dissent can trigger re-evaluation when it introduces a missing variable, evidence claim, constraint, or explicit alternative.
Executable review
A small Python model and machine-readable protocol make the assumptions inspectable and falsifiable.

Origin of inquiry

The project was triggered by a public observation from Christopher J. Skinner about enterprise AI's difficulty representing human thinking, communication, decision quality, and leadership. LDG treats that observation as a research question and develops an independent architecture around the decision lifecycle itself.

Research integrity

LDG v0.1 is a conceptual and executable working paper. It is not a validated safety standard, production governance product, or empirical proof. The included simulation exists so reviewers — human or AI — can challenge the logic, change assumptions, and construct counterexamples.
