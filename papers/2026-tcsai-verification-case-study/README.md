# TCSAI / NeuroSapiens+ Verification Case Study

**Status:** research dossier in progress  
**Investigator:** Amir Ahmadi  
**Date opened:** 22 August 2026  
**Repository:** Amir Ahmadi Research Papers

This folder preserves the research path that began as a LinkedIn disagreement about TCSAI / NeuroSapiens+ and developed into a reproducibility-oriented case study.

The project is deliberately structured so that a reader does not have to trust either Amir Ahmadi, Rafael Antonio (Tony) Cantero Suarez, or an AI-generated interpretation. Wherever possible, exact prompts, verbatim responses, screenshots, browser conditions, preservation hashes, and replication instructions are retained.

## Core question

> Which publicly observable claims of TCSAI can be independently reproduced, distinguished from ordinary software behavior, and connected to the mechanisms claimed to produce them?

## Research rule

This dossier separates four layers:

1. **What was claimed**
2. **What was asked**
3. **What was directly observed**
4. **What may and may not reasonably be inferred**

A failed test is not automatically treated as falsification of TCSAI as a whole. A successful test is not automatically treated as validation of its physical or ontological claims.

## Evidential status language

The dossier uses the following classifications:

- **Demonstrated**
- **Not demonstrated under tested conditions**
- **Observed claim-behavior tension**
- **Undetermined**
- **Requires independent external evidence**

The terms *fraud*, *fake*, or *deception* are not inferred merely from non-reproduction, because intent is a separate claim requiring separate evidence.

## Preservation note

The original Safari WebArchive, continuous screen recording, interface screenshots, LinkedIn screenshots, and derivative PDFs are preserved separately from the analytical text. Binary evidence will be indexed here together with cryptographic hashes. A derivative or screenshot documents what the interface displayed; it does not, by itself, prove the implementation of an undisclosed backend.

## Publication plan

This case study is intended to become the empirical companion to a broader paper on human-AI co-creation, epistemic responsibility, articulation, and the distinction between explanation and demonstration.

The reader-facing principle is simple:

> **Do not trust our interpretation. Reproduce the tests.**
