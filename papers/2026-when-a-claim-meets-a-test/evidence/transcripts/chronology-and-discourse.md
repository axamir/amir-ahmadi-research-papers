## Discourse analysis, separated from technical results

The public exchange included both methodological claims and personal/professional language. The former included requests for “real evidence,” a “final deduction,” and study on the framework’s own terms. The latter included statements about Amir’s reputation, credibility, professional status, ability to create comparable work, and a reference to criticism “before preschool.”

The only finding made here is textual: as scrutiny increased, parts of the exchange shifted from evaluating TCSAI’s claims toward evaluating the person asking questions. No clinical, motivational, or moral diagnosis is made. That shift does not alter the technical verdicts, which rely only on observable claims and interface behavior.

## Public-use and privacy boundary

Public posts may be quoted with their URLs and captures. Direct messages are used only where research-relevant and only as excerpts needed to establish scope or chronology. This draft does not treat the public challenge as blanket authorization to publish private correspondence, and it does not infer consent to a conclusion.
