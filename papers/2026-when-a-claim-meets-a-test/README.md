# When a Claim Meets a Test

## A Reproducible Case Study of TCSAI, Evidence, Falsifiability, and Human–AI Co-Inquiry

**Author:** Amir Ahmadi  
**Status:** Public research draft; published as a bounded, English-only research record  
**Study date:** 22 August 2026  
**Scope:** Public statements and interface behaviour available to the investigator; this is not an audit of undisclosed source code, infrastructure, finances, or persons.

This case study records what happened after Amir Ahmadi publicly asked whether claims associated with TCSAI could be independently examined. It follows the ensuing public and private discussion, the public invitation to reach a conclusion, and a documented session with the NeuroSapiens+/TCSAI interface.

The study uses a deliberately narrow rule: a statement is classified only as **Supported**, **Partially Supported**, **Not Demonstrated**, **Contradicted**, or **Not Testable from Available Evidence**. Adoption, a user report, or a useful output is not treated as validation of a claimed mechanism.

Generative AI assisted with organizing the record, drafting prose, and generating alternative questions. The human investigator selected the study questions, conducted or preserved the documented interface interactions, chose the evidentiary boundaries, and is responsible for every factual assertion and verdict. Model-generated prose is not treated as independent evidence about TCSAI.
